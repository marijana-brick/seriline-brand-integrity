# Seriline — observed public brand guidelines

**Document status:** PUBLIC-SOURCE INTERPRETATION · **Research date:** 2026-11-05 · **Approval:** Not reviewed by Seriline brand owners.

These are **working guidelines** for agents producing Seriline-facing work. They summarize observable content and design patterns from Seriline's public website and product materials, then translate those patterns into creative recommendations. They are **not** the company's official identity manual. Preserve the difference between **OBSERVED** (first-party source), **RECOMMENDED** (a reasonable application), and **UNVERIFIED** (requires the internal brand team).

## 1. Brand position and personality

**OBSERVED:** Seriline is a Swedish company specializing in identity and access management with software, web solutions, security hardware, credentials and services. The public site emphasizes managing people, credentials and assets through their lifecycle. It communicates security and integrity alongside simplified day-to-day workflows. [Homepage](https://www.seriline.com/en) · [About](https://www.seriline.com/en/about-us)

**RECOMMENDED creative north star:** *Make complex identity and access processes understandable, controlled and usable.*

Three working traits:
- **Assured:** measured technical confidence; claims earn trust through accuracy, not spectacle.
- **Practical:** show real products, real workflows and tangible operational outcomes.
- **Systematic:** precise hierarchies, consistent naming, clear relationships between people, credentials, permissions and assets.

Avoid interpretations that make Seriline look like a speculative AI startup, a dramatic cyber-threat consultancy or a generic lifestyle brand.

## 2. Visual identity

### Color

**OBSERVED:** Seriline's online product graphics and marketing imagery make prominent use of **blue**, often alongside **white** and dark neutrals. For example, the Serix IAM overview uses a dark-to-bright blue technical field; Serix Online diagrams use a vivid blue accent with white space and gray/black details. [Serix IAM](https://www.seriline.com/en/superior-system-serix-iam) · [Serix Online](https://www.seriline.com/en/ordering-portal-serix-online)

**RECOMMENDED:** Use blue as a recognizable brand-led anchor, white/light space for readability, and dark neutrals for headings and technical content. Treat diagrams, product brochures and website chrome as different contexts rather than forcing identical blue fills everywhere. Use color to clarify structure and action.

**UNVERIFIED:** Official blue HEX/RGB/CMYK/Pantone values; extended color palette; contrast tokens; product/UI semantic colors. **Do not invent or canonize numeric tokens.** Sample an approved source only for a provisional mockup and mark it as sampled, not official.

### Typography and hierarchy

**OBSERVED:** Public pages use direct, descriptive headings, product names and supporting explanatory copy. Product collateral prioritizes technical information over ornamental editorial treatments. [Homepage](https://www.seriline.com/en) · [Marketing materials](https://www.seriline.com/en/support/marketing-material)

**RECOMMENDED:** Use accessible, clear sans-serif typography with a deliberate headline/body distinction; compact, readable labels in tables and UI; short section headings; consistent capitalization within each channel. Support both English and Swedish content.

**UNVERIFIED:** Official type family, font weights, sizes, letter spacing, line heights and licensed font files. Never label a substitute typeface an approved Seriline font.

### Layout, spacing and shapes

**OBSERVED:** The website uses product/solution categories, section headings and explanatory modules; published technical diagrams make product and workflow relationships explicit. [Homepage](https://www.seriline.com/en) · [Serix IAM](https://www.seriline.com/en/superior-system-serix-iam)

**RECOMMENDED:** Favor ordered grids, clear section rhythm, informative whitespace and consistent alignment. Put one main thought or user task in each section. In UI, favor functional tables, filters, details, status and actions over decorative card walls. In marketing, mix strong product imagery with process diagrams and concise text.

**UNVERIFIED:** Official grid, spacing scale, radii, shadows, border widths, responsive breakpoints and UI component library. Use project standards where they exist.

### Logos and brand marks

**OBSERVED:** Seriline branding appears on credentials, card readers and published materials. [Product example](https://www.seriline.com/en/article/fido2-kit-reader-1-card-7361)

**RECOMMENDED:** Use original Seriline logo assets from approved sources. Maintain proportions, contrast and readable size. Never use an AI-redrawn wordmark, improvised icon or unapproved sub-brand lockup.

**UNVERIFIED:** Official clearspace, minimum size, monochrome use, placement rules, sub-brand relationship, background restrictions. Request these before production-critical applications.

## 3. Photography, illustrations and diagrams

**OBSERVED:** Public material includes recognizable **hardware** (Cidron readers, badges, cards, terminals), product screenshots and explanatory diagrams linking systems and credentials. Cidron is described as a robust, timeless reader family intended for real indoor and outdoor installations. [Cidron](https://www.seriline.com/en/cidron) · [Serix IAM](https://www.seriline.com/en/superior-system-serix-iam) · [Marketing materials](https://www.seriline.com/en/support/marketing-material)

**RECOMMENDED visual order:**
1. Approved real Seriline product photography and product UI screenshots.
2. Accurate diagrams of identity, reader, credential, approval and issuance workflows.
3. Authentic workplace contexts (reception, access point, administrative desk) with the right product depicted.
4. Generic conceptual visuals only when none of the above can explain the content.

Avoid anonymous padlocks, holographic shields, neon hacker tunnels, synthetic glowing fingerprint devices, floating 3D cubes and fabricated Cidron renders. Keep technical diagrams legible, not merely atmospheric. Label integrations and arrows accurately. Use blue diagram highlights where they help reveal flow; don't add decorative motion or gradients by default.

## 4. Voice, language and messaging

**OBSERVED:** Seriline's English site often starts from a concrete need, explains a solution and names the operational benefit: managing identities, integrating systems, ordering ID cards, registering visitors and supporting physical access. The tone is explanatory and product-oriented, though published wording and punctuation are not always consistent. [Identity management](https://www.seriline.com/en/identity-management) · [Access management](https://www.seriline.com/en/access-management) · [Visitor](https://www.seriline.com/en/visitor-management-system-visitor)

**RECOMMENDED voice:** Professional, approachable, technically precise, clear to non-specialists. Improve readability and grammar rather than copying every inconsistency in the website's English wording. Adapt terminology for security professionals versus reception and procurement teams.

Writing pattern: **problem → mechanism → useful result → proof or next step**.

Prefer:
- "Manage identities, credentials and access rights across connected systems."
- "Pre-register visitors and simplify check-in at reception."
- "Choose the credential and issuance method that fits the organization."

Avoid:
- "Revolutionize your digital transformation with next-generation synergy."
- "Unlock limitless security with a seamless AI-powered ecosystem."
- "100% secure", "unhackable", "military-grade" or other unsupported guarantees.
- Overuse of "cutting-edge", "game-changing", "empower", "elevate" and generic three-part slogans.

Those examples are **new illustrative copy**, not official Seriline slogans. Use the source content to validate any specific feature claim.

### Naming and terms

**OBSERVED:** Use product names as shown by official sources: **Seriline**, **Serix IAM**, **Serix Online**, **Serix ID / Serix iD** (capitalization varies in public material; verify the current preferred spelling), **Serix IoT**, **Visitor**, **Cidron**, **PhotoID** and **FIDO2**. [Homepage](https://www.seriline.com/en) · [Marketing materials](https://www.seriline.com/en/support/marketing-material)

**RECOMMENDED:** Keep product names consistent within the deliverable, following the latest approved product document. Distinguish identity management, physical access control, visitor management, credential issuance and reader hardware. Do not invent umbrella products or features.

## 5. Design for common outputs

### Websites and landing pages
- Lead with a specific audience problem or workflow, not a vague "future of security" headline.
- Show the actual product/solution early and give the reader an understandable path to details, specifications or contact.
- Organize content by outcome, system capability, integrations and concrete next action.
- Product shots or workflow visuals should do more work than abstract stock imagery.

### Product interfaces and admin dashboards
- Lead with tasks: identities, credentials, access rights, visitors, approvals, issuance and status.
- Use predictable navigation, dense-but-readable tables, filters, search, explicit permissions and helpful empty states.
- Make success, warning and destructive actions unmistakable. Use accessible text labels, not color alone.
- Do not redesign an existing Serix/Visitor interface from public site impressions when its actual design system is available.

### Presentations, proposals and documents
- Prefer approved slide/document templates, if available.
- Present use cases as **current situation → workflow → system components → operational result**.
- Favor real diagrams, supported compatibility information and current device photography.
- Use concise headlines, clear data labels, restrained callouts and consistent product naming.

### Social, campaigns and imagery
- Show who uses the solution and where: access point, reception, administrator, credential holder.
- Anchor benefits in real product behavior, not AI-futurism.
- Keep device models and credentials faithful to approved source images.

## 6. Product guardrails

- **Serix IAM:** identity, credential, access authorization and asset-related management; verify each supported integration and deployment claim before publication. [Serix IAM](https://www.seriline.com/en/superior-system-serix-iam)
- **Serix Online:** online ordering of ID-related materials and related fulfillment; verify specific lead times, prices and approval paths. [Serix Online](https://www.seriline.com/en/ordering-portal-serix-online)
- **Visitor:** registration, pre-booking, reception and visitor check-in/out use cases; verify module and licensing scope. [Visitor](https://www.seriline.com/en/visitor-management-system-visitor)
- **Cidron:** physical card reader family; verify reader variants, RFID/NFC/BLE/QR functionality per model rather than implying every reader has every feature. [Cidron](https://www.seriline.com/en/cidron)

Security, privacy, GDPR, certification, interoperability and performance claims need a named current source and appropriate internal approval. A feature that assists GDPR processes is not proof of blanket legal compliance.

## 7. Quick anti-slop checklist

Before publishing, ask:
- Would this still look like Seriline if the logo were hidden?
- Are the visual choices traceable to Seriline's published brand or a deliberate, justified extension?
- Does the imagery depict real products or correct workflows?
- Are the claims specific, useful and sourced?
- Does the copy sound like a knowledgeable identity/access specialist, not a generic SaaS assistant?
- Do interface elements genuinely work, and are charts, labels and layouts readable?
- Are unverified choices and legal/security claims clearly marked?

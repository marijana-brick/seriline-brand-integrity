# Medium-specific brand checks

## Web and app UI
- Reuse native design tokens, components, breakpoints and interactions.
- Audit information architecture and user task before styling.
- Check keyboard navigation, color contrast, responsive behavior, loading/empty/error states, real content and icon consistency.
- Prefer actual product context over decorative visuals; do not 'fix' authentic brand gradients merely because they are gradients.

## Slides
- Use approved master/layouts; maintain consistent title baseline, grid, visual density and footers.
- One argument per slide, with specific evidence. Avoid generic stock photo + 3 bullets formula throughout.
- Check legibility at presentation size, hierarchy, chart units/source labels, and final exported rendering.

## Reports, proposals and documents
- Use company template, typography, headers, page styles and citation conventions.
- Replace vague executive summaries with decision-useful analysis; make headings informative, not ornamental.
- Verify pagination, tables, bullets, widows/orphans, accessible reading structure and export.

## Email and sales collateral
- Use actual customer context and approved claims. Sound like a human company representative, not an AI funnel.
- Make subject, first line and CTA specific. Keep length fit for audience and channel.
- Confirm sender identity, audience and sending permissions; draft only unless authorized to send.

## Social, campaigns and imagery
- Pull creative direction from positioning, audience and brand asset library.
- Demand a concept, not merely a 'premium' image treatment. Use approved logo and treatments.
- Avoid synthetic fake customer screenshots, fabricated logos and unlicensed imagery.
- Verify cropping, legibility, platform specs and accessibility metadata.

## Dashboards, charts and spreadsheets
- Prefer information design over decorative dashboards. Establish hierarchy from the decision and metric definitions.
- Use brand colors semantically and consistently, preserve standard warning/success distinctions.
- Label units, periods and provenance; do not misrepresent uncertainty or truncate axes misleadingly.
- Test formulas and inspect layout in the native file.

## Cross-tool transfer
- Preserve meaning and tokens when moving between tools, but adapt composition to the medium.
- Do not force identical layout across slide, web, spreadsheet and email; seek a recognizable family resemblance.
- Never pretend a tool can apply design tokens if it cannot. Deliver exportable values and documented substitutions.
